## 17. Performance Management & PIP Process
* **Definitions:**
* *Key Performance Indicator (KPI):* Quantifiable operational metrics tracking specific departmental output success.
* *Performance Improvement Plan (PIP):* A formal, structured remedial monitoring window triggered by chronic metric deficiencies.
* **Eligibility Rules & Scope:** Applies globally to all non-probationary permanent and part-time personnel operating across the organization.
* **Responsibility Matrix:** Line managers configure KPIs and lead tracking assessments. Employees document personal task achievements. Human Resources registers performance logs and mediates PIP structures.
* **Approval Workflows:** KPI alignment at start of cycle -> Bi-annual review calibration signed by manager and employee -> PIP initiation requiring dual authorization from the Department Head and the HR Director.
* **Operational Procedures:**
* Performance cycles follow an annual structure, segmented into Q1 goal alignment, mid-year progression reviews, and year-end comprehensive objective assessments.
* Evaluation matrices use balanced scorecards tracking domain-specific technical output alongside alignment with corporate values and behavioral standards.
* If an employee’s performance does not meet established KPI standards, a formal Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) will be initialized. The PIP is an explicit, written restorative window lasting 30, 60, or a maximum of 90 calendar days, outlining clear, measurable operational targets that must be restored.
* During an active PIP, regular performance alignment meetings occur between the manager and employee to log metric adjustments.
* If the employee satisfies all written PIP criteria by the terminal date of the plan, they are officially returned to good standing. If performance metrics are not restored within the allocated tracking period, the relationship will be evaluated under the corrective guidance or role reassignment framework.
